## What is a good research question

Coming up with a question is easy!  What is $$2+2$$? is a question, but I think we'd all agree it's not very good.
Figuring out a **good** research question is much harder, and is a skill that researchers develop over time, most often under the guidance of a mentor who helps them find some good questions to work on first.

To figure out what a good question is made up of, it helps to think about what bad questions are.  Most questions that are "bad" suffer from one or more of the following traits
* the question is too easy to answer, so answering it isn't very interesting;
* the question is too difficult to make any progress on at all;
* the question is ambiguous or not well-posed;
* the question is ill-suited to the researcher or research team.

So a good research question should be suited to the researcher's skills and knowledge, and should be at a happy medium between being so easy to answer that it is boring or so difficult to answer that there's no clear way to make progress.
A good rule of thumb is that it should be a question with many possible directions, so that even if you don't completely answer the question, you are able to answer a related one.

## Example question

Maybe our starting question is:

**Question:** What is the maximum number of moves required to solve any Rubik's Cube puzzle?

This is a difficult question that has no immediately obvious answer.  However, it has the markings of a good problem:
* We can try to shuffle a Rubik's cube and then figure out how to solve it, getting our hands on some good data immediately.
* We can start to pick up some patterns about the Rubik's Cube, such as what moves to do in various situations.
* We can also search online for additional ideas (but we need to make sure to remember and **cite our sources**!)

After some time, we maybe hit a wall in getting closer to an answer.  However, we look online and we find that the answer is $$20$$, which researchers answered via computer simulations taking roughly $$21$$ years of CPU cycles.  Not something that we can do ... plus the problem is already solved!

Don't panic, this happens all the time.

What we do now is change the question based on our experience.  Maybe we can ask instead:

**New Question:** If you arrange the stickers randomly on a Rubik's cube by peeling them off and putting them back on, could the cube become unsolvable?  When would the cube still be solvable?

Or else we can ask

**New Question:** Suppose we don't want to solve the Cube, but instead we want to go from one specific Cube position to another.  What is the minimum number of moves?  How can we show that it is the minimum number of moves?

Or how about:

**New Question:** Suppose that we have two Rubik's cubes which are all mixed up.  Suppose that whatever move we do to one Cube, we also have to do to the other.  When is there a set of moves that will solve both Cubes?

## Question list

Below is a list of research questions to choose from.
You may also choose your own research question, though you are required to get approval from me.

* (1) How many possible ways are there to place the entire fleet in a standard game of Battleship?

* (2) How many ways are there to completely fill up a Battleship board with $$50$$ $$2\times 1$$ ships.

* (3) Suppose you are playing against a computer which always shoots at the most likely ship position based on the assumption that the fleet was randomly generated.  What fleet position avoids the computer getting a hit as long as possible.

* (4) We can represent a Battleship board as a $$10\times 10$$ matrix $$A$$ whose entries are $$0$$'s and $$1$$'s.  Suppose that we know the sum of the ten rows, ten columns, ten diagonals, and ten antidiagonals.  Is it possible to reconstruct the matrix $$A$$?  Is there more than one solution?

* (5) Suppose you play Clobber on a graph.  What types of graphs guarantee a win for Player 1?

* (6) Create a version of Chomp that you can play on certain directed graphs.  For what kinds of graphs does Player 1 still have a "strategy stealing argument" that shows they can force a win?  For which graphs can you work out a winning strategy for Player 1?

* (7) What if you play Chomp, but with some squares missing so that the chocolate bar has a hole in it.  This makes certain bites impossible, since there's no square to bite.  How does this change the game?  Under what conditions is Player 1 still guaranteed a win?

* (8) Suppose you are playing Minesweeper on a $$n\times n$$ grid with $$m$$ mines and make the first click.  How many mine configurations are consistent with the revealed pattern?  Can we determine statistics applicable to the real game?

* (9) In the game Break the Cube, figure out how many ways there are to arrange the pieces.  

* (10) In the card game War, is it possible for a game to go on forever?  What is the average length of a game?

* (11) Can you find a way to label every square in a $$10\times 10$$ grid with the letters $$A-J$$ and the numbers $$0-9$$ so that: every square gets both a letter and a number; each letter and number appears in each row and each column; no letter-number combination is repeated?  Is it then possible to color the squares with ten different colors so that each color appears once in each row and column and no letter-number-color combination is repeated?

* (12) Let $$t_0, t_1, t_2, t_3, \dots$$ be the Thue-Morse binary sequence.  Determine the value of the infinite sum

$$t_0-t_1+\frac{1}{2}t_2-\frac{1}{3}t_3+\frac{1}{4}t_4-\frac{1}{5}t_5+\dots$$

Are there any other infinite sums involving $$t_n$$ that you can figure out the value of?
For example, can you figure out

$$t_0+\frac{1}{2}t_1+\frac{1}{4}t_2+\frac{1}{8}t_3+\frac{1}{16}t_4+\frac{1}{32}t_5+\dots$$
 
* (13)  Let $$a,b,c$$ be integers and consider the function  $$f: \mathbb{Z}^2\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}$$ defined by the rule

$$f(x,y) = ax + by + cxy.$$

For which values of $$a,b,c$$ does the function turn out to be surjective?

* (14)  Are there any triples of integers $$(x,y,z)$$ satisfying the equation

$$x^2+y^2=(yz-1)^3?$$

What are all the solutions?

* (15)  What is the minimum value of the expression

$$|a+b+c|\left(\frac{1}{|a-b|} + \frac{1}{|b-c|} + \frac{1}{|c-a|}\right)$$

over all possible triples of distinct real numbers $$a,b,c$$ satisfying the identity

$$a^2+b^2+c^2 = 2(ab + bc + ca).$$
